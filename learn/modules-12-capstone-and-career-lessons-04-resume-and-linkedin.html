Lesson 04 — Résumé and LinkedIn

Time: ~3 hours · Core

The résumé

One page (two if you have many years of relevant experience). For each project or role, write impact, not activities:

WeakStrong
Built a RAG chatbot with FastAPI and ReactBuilt a course assistant that answers students' questions from the institute's documents with cited sources; 90% correct on a 52-question evaluation; every out-of-scope question declined
Wrote a data pipelineCombined three lead sources and an admissions export, matching 22 of 25 enrolments and showing conversion by channel for the first time
Worked with stakeholdersRan discovery with five stakeholders; reframed a "chatbot" request into a follow-up-speed problem with a measurable target

Structure: summary (2 lines: what kind of FDE, what you've built) → projects/experience → skills (grouped: languages, data, cloud, AI, integration) → education and certifications.

Tailor it per application: mirror the job description's language where it's true.

LinkedIn

  • Headline: "Forward Deployed Engineer · AI and data applications · Python, FastAPI, PostgreSQL, LLMs" (or the role you're targeting)
  • About: three short paragraphs: what you do, what you've built (with numbers), what you're looking for
  • Featured: your flagship project, a write-up or a recorded demo
  • Posts: short write-ups of what you learned (an incident, an evaluation surprise). They show communication skill, which FDE hiring values.

Honesty

Describe the handbook projects truthfully: AdmitFlow is a guided learning project with synthetic data; your capstone is independent; name real users only with their permission. Interviewers will ask detailed questions; inflated claims fall apart in minutes, and honest ones with clear judgement stand out.

Check yourself

Rewrite three résumé bullets using the impact pattern, then ask someone in an FDE, solutions or AI-engineering role to review your résumé against a real job description.
