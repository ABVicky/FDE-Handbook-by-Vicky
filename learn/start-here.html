Start Here: how to use this handbook

This page takes about 10 minutes to read. It will save you hours of feeling lost.

When people open a big learning repository for the first time, they usually see dozens of folders and files and think, "Where do I even begin?" So let me make it simple.

The only rule: follow the path

You don't need to read this repository from top to bottom. You only need to do one module at a time, in order, starting with Module 01.

Every page ends with a "Next:" link. Follow it, and you'll never be lost.

What's inside every module

Each module has the same parts, so once you've done one, you'll know your way around all of them. Here's what each part is, in plain words:

PartWhat it isDo I need it?
📖 LessonsShort readings that explain one idea at a time, with examplesYes. This is where you learn
✅ CheckpointA short quiz. If you already know the topic, pass it and move onOptional: a shortcut if you're experienced
✏️ ExercisesSmall practice tasks, with answersYes. Try them before looking at the answers
🏗️ BuildThe next piece of the AdmitFlow project, built by youYes. This is the most important part
🔧 Break & fixSomething that's broken on purpose. You find out why and fix itYes. Real jobs are full of this
🤝 Field TrackA task about people, not code: an interview, an email, a demoYes. This is what makes you an FDE
📚 ResourcesExtra reading and official documentationOnly when you want to go deeper

A simple routine for each module: read the lessons → do the exercises → do the build → do the break & fix → do the Field Track → tick the "Done when" list at the bottom of the module page.

What you can safely ignore (for now)

Seeing lots of things you don't understand yet is normal. These can wait:

  • Anything marked Stretch. It's optional extra depth. Skip it on your first pass.
  • The projects/admitflow/ folder. That's my finished version of the project. Use it to compare after you've tried building each piece yourself, not before. It contains code from all 12 modules, so most of it won't make sense until you get there.
  • Folders like .github/, scripts/ and templates/. They're tools. I'll link to them when you need them.
  • ROADMAP.md. It's the detailed plan of the whole course. Useful as a reference, but you don't need to read it to start.

A realistic weekly rhythm

If you study 15–20 hours a week:

  • 3–4 days: lessons and exercises (1–2 hours a day)
  • One longer session (4–6 hours): the build
  • One short session: the Field Track task or the break & fix

Most modules take 1–5 weeks. It's completely fine to be slower. Understanding matters more than speed.

When you get stuck

You will get stuck sometimes. Everyone does, including experienced engineers. Here's what to do:

  1. Read the error message slowly, word by word. It usually tells you what's wrong.
  2. Look at the hints. Most exercises and every break & fix have hints you can open one at a time.
  3. Search for the exact error text online. This is a real professional skill, not cheating.
  4. Check the troubleshooting list: it lists real errors I hit while building this, and the fixes.
  5. Then look at the solution, and make sure you understand why it works.
