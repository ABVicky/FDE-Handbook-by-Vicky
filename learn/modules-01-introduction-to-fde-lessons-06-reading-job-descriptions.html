Lesson 06 — Reading FDE job descriptions

Time: ~2 hours · Core

Job descriptions are the best free data you have about what companies want. They also show which version of "FDE" a company means, and that varies a lot.

Step 1: Find where the role sits on the spectrum

Most FDE roles fall somewhere between these two ends:

Engineering-heavy FDEConsulting-heavy FDE
"Ship production code," "own deployments end to end""Advise customers," "lead workshops"
Lists specific languages and systemsLists "technical aptitude" in general terms
Coding interview mentionedCase interview or presentation mentioned
Reports into engineeringReports into customer success, services or sales
Success = systems running in productionSuccess = customer satisfaction, renewals

Neither is better. They suit different people. Know which one you're applying for.

Step 2: Decode the common phrases

PhraseWhat it usually means
"Comfortable with ambiguity"Requirements will be unclear; you'll have to find out what to build
"Own outcomes end to end"You're accountable if it doesn't work in production, not just for your code
"Customer-facing" / "work directly with customers"Meetings, demos, maybe travel; communication is assessed in interviews
"Up to X% travel"Take the number seriously
"Production experience"You've shipped and maintained something real users depended on
"Familiarity with LLMs, RAG, agents"AI FDE role: expect Module 08 topics in interviews
"Experience with enterprise systems / integrations"Identity, permissions, CRMs/ERPs, messy APIs (Module 09)
"Strong fundamentals"Coding interviews: data structures, debugging, system design
"Startup mindset" / "scrappy"Small team, little process, you'll wear many hats

Step 3: Separate must-haves from wish lists

Job descriptions often list far more than any one person has. A rough rule:

  • Repeated across many postings → core skill; learn it deeply
  • Specific to one company's product (e.g. their own platform) → learned on the job
  • Long lists of tools ("Kafka, Airflow, Snowflake, Databricks, Spark…") → they want one or two plus the ability to learn the rest

This is why the roadmap splits technologies into Core and Optional.

Step 4: Build your own skills table

Exercise 03 has you collect five real postings and fill in a table like this:

CompanyEngineering ↔ consulting (1–5)LanguagesCloudAI skillsIntegration skillsClient skillsTravelInterview hints
…

Do this again every few months. It's the fastest way to see where the market is moving, and it's how this repository keeps itself up to date (see ROADMAP §16).

Where to find postings

  • Company careers pages (most reliable)
  • LinkedIn and other job boards: search "Forward Deployed Engineer", "Forward Deployed Software Engineer", "Deployed Engineer", "Field Engineer (AI)", "Solutions Engineer (Deployment)"
  • Save a dated copy in resources/job-descriptions/. Postings disappear quickly.

Check yourself

Find one FDE posting today. Rate it 1 (engineering-heavy) to 5 (consulting-heavy) and write two sentences explaining why.
