Client-scenario interviews

The interviewer plays a client. They're checking whether you ask before you build, stay calm, and communicate clearly. Practise with a partner using these scripts. The client reads only their brief; the observer scores with the rubric.

Scenario 1 — "We need an AI chatbot"

Client brief: You run a 40-branch pharmacy chain. You saw a competitor's chatbot. You're vague about the problem. If asked good questions, reveal: the real pain is pharmacists spending 2 hours a day on phone calls asking "is my prescription ready?"; your system has the status; you're worried about health data. What good looks like: asks what problem the chatbot would solve, for whom, how it's done today, volumes, data and privacy constraints; proposes something narrow (SMS when a prescription is ready, or a status lookup) before any chatbot; names a success metric.

Scenario 2 — "It's broken and the board meets tomorrow"

Client brief: You're the director. The report your FDE built shows enrolments down 30% this month. You're angry and want it fixed tonight. (The real cause, revealed only if asked: a data feed stopped two weeks ago.) What good looks like: stays calm; acknowledges; asks when it started and what changed; checks data freshness before concluding anything; gives a time for the next update; doesn't promise a cause before knowing it.

Scenario 3 — "Just add this small thing"

Client brief: You're the head of operations. Two weeks before go-live, you want three new features "that should be quick." You'll accept trade-offs if they're explained well. What good looks like: listens, writes them down, estimates impact, offers options (phase 2, swap, move date), lets the client decide, confirms in writing.

Scenario 4 — "Your security review failed"

Client brief: You're the client's CISO. You found that the vendor's AI tool sends customer emails to an AI provider in another country. You want to know what data, why, and what they'll do. What good looks like: doesn't get defensive; asks what the policy requires; explains exactly what data flows where; offers concrete options (region-pinned hosting, minimization, redaction, an in-network model); proposes a timeline.

Observer rubric (1–3 each)

Asked before proposing
Found the real problem
Quantified something (volume, time, cost)
Offered options with trade-offs
Clear, jargon-free, calm
Ended with a concrete next step
