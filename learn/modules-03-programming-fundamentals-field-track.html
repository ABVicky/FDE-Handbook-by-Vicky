Module 03 — Field Track: Weekly status updates

Time: ~30 minutes a week for weeks 3–6 · Capabilities: C5

Why

On an engagement, the client can't see your code. They see your updates. A short, honest, well-structured weekly update is one of the most effective trust-building tools an FDE has. It surfaces risks early, gets decisions made, and means nobody is surprised.

The task

Every week while you work on the build, write a status update to Mr. Fernandes (Head of Admissions), copying Ms. Rao (Director). Save them in my-work/field-track/m03-status-week-N.md. Write at least two.

Template

**AdmitFlow, weekly update, week N** · Status: 🟢 On track / 🟡 At risk / 🔴 Blocked

**Done this week**
- (outcomes the client cares about, not tasks: "Ad and website enquiries now combine into one list")

**Next week**
- ...

**Risks and blockers**
- (what could stop us, and what we're doing about it)

**Decisions needed from you**
- (a specific question, options, and a recommended answer, with a date you need it by)

**Numbers**
- (e.g. "190 unique students from 205 enquiries; 15 duplicates removed")

Make it realistic

Include at least one of these situations in your updates, as if it really happened:

  • You found that 29 of 60 ad leads were being dropped by the old cleaning script (this module's break & fix). How do you tell them without blaming anyone, and what do you need them to do?
  • You need a decision on the dedupe rule: should two people with the same phone but different names be merged? Give options and a recommendation.
  • Something took longer than expected (e.g. walk-in dates were day-first). Say so plainly, and say what it means for the timeline.

Rules

  • ≤ 200 words. Busy people read the first three lines.
  • Outcomes, not activity. "Wrote importers.py" means nothing to the director. "Counsellors can now search all three channels at once" does.
  • No surprises. Bad news early, with a plan.
  • One clear ask. If you need a decision, make it easy to say yes.

Done when

  • [ ] Two or more updates, each ≤ 200 words
  • [ ] At least one contains a risk, and one contains a decision request with a recommendation
  • [ ] A peer (or a non-technical friend) read one and could tell you, in one sentence, where the project stands
