Module 01 — Solutions

These are model answers. Yours can differ and still be right, as long as the reasoning holds.

Lesson 03 check yourself

  1. Solutions engineer: built a pre-sale demo to win the deal.
  2. FDE: production pipeline plus alerting, in the customer's context.
  3. Technical PM: deciding product priorities.
  4. AI / ML engineer: model quality work on the product.

Exercise 01 — Classify the role

  1. FDE: integration inside the customer's environment plus user training.
  2. Solutions engineer: pre-sale demo and security questions.
  3. Implementation consultant: configuring existing software to a spec.
  4. AI engineer (or software engineer): product-wide model improvement, no specific customer.
  5. Technical PM: customer research feeding a product spec.
  6. FDE: adapted to customer constraints, deployed, measured business outcome.
  7. Software engineer: internal product code quality.
  8. FDE: handover plus feeding reusable work back to the product.

Bonus: #1 could also be a strong implementation consultant if no custom code was involved. #4 depends on whether "ranking model" work is ML (AI engineer) or systems optimization (software engineer). Role boundaries blur in real companies.

Tackle first: permissions and outdated documents. A wrong or leaked answer destroys trust before anything else matters.

Exercise 04 — Spot the bad problem statement

1. "We need an AI chatbot for our website." Wrong: names a solution; no who, no cost, no success metric. Ask: What do visitors need that they can't get today? What happens to enquiries now? What does a lost enquiry cost? Rewrite: "Prospective students who enquire through the website outside office hours (≈ 35% of enquiries, assumption) wait an average of 18 hours for a reply, and about half never hear back. We'll consider this solved when 90% of enquiries get an accurate answer or a booked call within 1 hour, without adding staff."

2. "Our processes are inefficient…" Wrong: vague; no specific process, user or measure. Ask: Which process costs the most time or money? Who does it? How often? Rewrite: pick one concrete process, e.g. "Finance staff spend ~25 hours a week re-typing supplier invoices into the ERP…", with baseline and target.

3. "Customer support is slow." Wrong: no measure, no baseline, no cause. Ask: Slow how: first response, resolution time? For which request types? What's the baseline? Rewrite: "Median first response time for 'where is my order' tickets (60% of volume) is 9 hours. Target: under 30 minutes within 3 months, with customer-satisfaction score unchanged or better."

4. "Build a dashboard showing all the data…" Wrong: names a solution; "all the data" is unbounded scope; "better decisions" isn't measurable. Ask: Which decisions? Made by whom, how often? What do they use today? What went wrong last time? Rewrite: "The operations director decides weekly staffing using a spreadsheet assembled manually over 6 hours from three systems; it's often a week out of date. Solved when the director gets an automatically refreshed staffing view every Monday by 9am, accurate to within 2% of source systems."

5. "…99.9% accuracy by next month." Wrong: prescribes the technique (ML); the target and timeline are arbitrary and probably unrealistic; there's no baseline time or current error rate. Ask: What's the current processing time and error rate? What does an error cost? Is human review acceptable for uncertain cases? Rewrite: "Accounts-payable staff take a median of 12 minutes per invoice, with a 3% keying-error rate. Target: median under 3 minutes and errors ≤ 1%, using automated extraction plus human review of low-confidence fields, measured over a 4-week pilot."
