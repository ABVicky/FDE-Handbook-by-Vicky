# Module 08 — Field Track: Demo the assistant, including where it fails

**Time:** ~2 hours (prepare 1 h, demo 30 min, follow-up 30 min) · **Capabilities:** C5

## Why

AI demos create expectations fast. A demo that only shows successes sets up disappointment later. A demo that shows **where it fails, and what happens then**, builds trust and leads to realistic acceptance criteria, which you can then measure.

## The task

Run a 20-minute demo for "Ms. Rao and Mr. Fernandes" (two peers role-playing; brief them to be sceptical).

**Prepare:**
1. Five questions it handles well (fees, timings, campus, refunds, evening classes)
2. Two it declines correctly ("PhD in astrophysics", "guarantee me a scholarship")
3. One it gets wrong today (pick a real failure from the evaluation)
4. The draft workflow: generate a draft for a lead, show it's pending, approve it
5. One slide: the evaluation numbers in plain words ("47 of 52 test questions fully right; every fee question right; every out-of-scope question declined")

**Demo:** show all of the above, *including the failure*, and explain what the student sees when it fails.

**Agree acceptance criteria** with them, e.g.:
- every fee question correct (13/13), enforced automatically
- ≥ 90% of the evaluation set correct before public launch
- 100% of out-of-scope questions declined
- no message sent without approval
- a review of 50 real questions after the first two weeks

**Follow-up email (≤ 150 words):** what you showed, the agreed criteria, next steps and dates.

## Done when

- [ ] Demo includes at least one real failure, shown deliberately
- [ ] Acceptance criteria written down and agreed
- [ ] Follow-up email sent to the role-players; they confirm it matches what was agreed
