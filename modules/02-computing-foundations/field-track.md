# Module 02 — Field Track: Explain it to a non-technical person

**Time:** ~1.5 hours · **Capabilities:** C5

## Why

Clients will ask you things like "Why can't your system just connect to our server?" or "Why is it slow today?" The director, the finance manager and the counsellors don't need jargon. They need an accurate picture they can act on. Explaining simply is a skill you can practise.

## The task

**Part 1: write it (30 min).** In **200 words or fewer**, explain to Ms. Rao (the institute's director) what happens between a student typing `northbridge.example` into their phone and the website appearing.

Rules:
- No unexplained jargon. If you use a word like "server" or "DNS," explain it in the same sentence or don't use it.
- Use **one** analogy at most, and make sure it's accurate.
- End with one sentence on why this matters to *her* (e.g. why the site might be slow or unreachable, and who fixes what).

**Part 2: test it (30 min).** Give your explanation to a real non-technical person. Then ask them to explain it back to you in their own words, without looking. Note:
- Which parts they got right
- Which parts they got wrong or skipped
- Any words they asked about

**Part 3: revise it (30 min).** Rewrite your explanation based on what you learned. Save both versions in `my-work/field-track/m02-explanation.md`, with a short note on what you changed and why.

## Done when

- [ ] Version 1 is ≤ 200 words
- [ ] You tested it on a real person and recorded what they understood
- [ ] Version 2 fixes the misunderstandings you found

## What good looks like (an excerpt, not the full answer)

> "When a student types our web address, their phone first looks it up, like finding a phone number in a contacts list, to get the address of the computer that stores our website. It then connects to that computer, sets up a private, locked connection so nobody can read what's sent, and asks for the home page…"

Notice: no "TCP," no "TLS handshake," but nothing false either.
