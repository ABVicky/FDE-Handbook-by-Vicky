# Module 11 — Checkpoint

Pass mark: **8 / 10**, plus the [build](build.md) and [break & fix](break-and-fix/README.md).

---

**1.** Name three kinds of value software can create for a client, with an AdmitFlow example and a way to measure each.

**2.** What belongs in the "stops" column of a future-state process, and why does it matter?

**3.** Two senior stakeholders want different things from phase 2. What do you do in the meeting?

**4.** A new feature request arrives two weeks before go-live. Describe your response.

**5.** Why does AdmitFlow's ROI model use contribution instead of revenue, and a `causal_share`?

**6.** The first version of a business case shows payback in under a month. What should you do?

**7.** Rewrite for an executive: "We refactored the ingestion pipeline to improve dedupe precision across heterogeneous sources."

**8.** List the four layers of "done" and give a criterion for each.

**9.** Name four change-management actions that would have prevented the break & fix situation.

**10.** What must be true before you can say an engagement is handed over?

---

<details>
<summary><strong>Answers</strong></summary>

1. Revenue (more enrolments; conversion before/after), cost (hours not spent copying; hours × cost, or capacity freed), risk (no wrong fees quoted, fewer data incidents; complaints and incidents).
2. The old activities each role stops doing (personal spreadsheets, the Friday report). If old ways aren't removed, people do both, then drop the new one.
3. Restate each goal measurably, find the shared outcome, show trade-offs explicitly, and let the sponsor decide, in writing.
4. Write it down, estimate impact, offer options (add it and move the date, swap for something, or phase 2), let the sponsor decide, record the decision.
5. Extra fees aren't extra profit (teaching costs); and the historical link between speed and enrolment is correlation, so only part of it is caused by speed.
6. Look for what's missing: hidden costs (extra staff), margin vs revenue, causality, capacity, seasonality. Then lead with a conservative case and a pilot.
7. "AdmitFlow now spots the same student across all enquiry channels more reliably, so counsellors call each student once."
8. Delivered (features tested), adopted (all users active weekly, old process retired), effective (the outcome metric moved for a full cycle), owned (the client runs and supports it without you).
9. Retire the Friday spreadsheet; meet the manager's reporting need in the product; train each person in their context (including late joiners and reception); fix mobile usability; provide a clear support channel; measure adoption weekly after go-live.
10. Named client owners for use, operations, content and budget; documents they've tested; credentials transferred and yours removed; a support model agreed; outcomes reported against agreed metrics.

</details>
